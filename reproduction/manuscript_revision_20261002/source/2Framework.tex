\subsection{Task Definition and Label Schema}

Given a Chinese job-ad sentence $x$, the task is to predict non-overlapping spans $(s,e,t)$. Here, $s$ and $e$ are zero-based, end-exclusive Unicode-code-point offsets, and $t\in\{L,K,S,T\}$. Each span must reproduce the contiguous substring $x[s:e]$, including its spelling and internal spaces. The annotation describes a requirement in the advertisement; it does not measure an applicant's ability.

The handbook assigns L to language requirements, including language names, proficiency levels, examinations, and certificates. K covers facts, principles, theories, and fields of study. S covers occupational actions, methods, and tool use. T covers transversal capabilities such as communication, cooperation, cognition, and self-management. These categories draw on ESCO and on the distinction between knowledge and skills in the European Qualifications Framework~\citep{ESCOv1_2_2024,council2017eqf}.

By project convention, educational and non-language qualifications also receive K. They serve as recruitment proxies for knowledge requirements, so K counts combine substantive knowledge mentions with qualification proxies. We have not established separate proxy counts or evaluated the task after excluding them. The rules below explain how context determines type and how experience requirements are handled. Evaluation concerns mention boundaries and LSKT types.

\subsection{Annotation Guidelines and Adjudication}
\label{sec:annotation-guidelines-r36}

The guidelines address three decisions that a list of skill terms cannot settle: whether a clause is eligible, how much text to retain, and which type fits its context. Annotation begins by determining whether a clause expresses a requirement for the candidate. Benefits, applicant-side procedures, company promotion, and pure outcomes do not qualify on their own; recruitment activities performed as occupational duties are assessed separately. Mixed clauses are considered separately. A sentence with no competency mention receives an empty annotation; insufficient context or an unresolved interpretation is recorded as uncertainty.

We adapted SkillSpan's semantic-completeness and coordination principles for Chinese annotation~\citep{zhang2022skillspan}. Our handbook asks annotators to select the shortest source span that preserves the complete requirement, without a fixed length cap. Generic proficiency triggers usually remain outside the span, but a modal can remain inside a complete colloquial capability expression. Necessary action heads and complements are retained. Coordinated activities are split only when each remains interpretable; a shared action and its objects stay together (Figure~\ref{fig:illustrative-annotations}).

Experience requires a separate check; generic work experience and duration alone are excluded. Explicit industry-background requirements use domain cores as broad-K proxies under the project convention. For occupational activities, the experience qualifier is omitted only if the remaining span preserves the requirement. It can stay outside coordinated items if each remains interpretable in context. Neither conjunctions nor experience qualifiers alone determine boundaries.

Type depends on what the clause requires: using SQL supports S, whereas knowledge of SQL principles supports K. A predicate can determine type without being part of the selected span. Familiarity alone does not decide type. General communication and coordination remain T inside duty clauses, whereas technical operations receive S. Language examinations receive L; educational and non-language qualifications receive K. Figure~\ref{fig:illustrative-annotations} shows original corpus examples with their archived annotations, and \hyperref[app:a]{Appendix A} gives further examples and rule sources.

Adjudication treats scope, boundaries, and type as separate decisions~\citep{artstein2008agreement}. There is no total priority order among L, K, S, and T. Under the current handbook, a mention with settled scope and boundaries but unresolved type may receive provisional S in the adjudication record, with alternatives logged. The handbook requires exclusion of the whole unresolved record from final training targets; provisional S is not an accepted target. Same-boundary alternatives, nested candidates, and unresolved overlaps are kept in the adjudication record rather than duplicated in the flat annotation layer. \hyperref[app:a]{Appendix A} gives the full rules and their history. Each completed experiment retains the labels assigned under its original handbook.

\subsection{Human Annotation and LLM-Assisted Labeling}
\label{sec:annotation-workflow-r36}

Human coding informed the handbook and frozen reference. Official OpenAI Codex generated the adopted Silver labels. Initial sample review preceded bulk annotation. Automated checks verified substrings, offsets, labels, and coverage; human review addressed interpretation.

Bulk annotation used the handbook available at generation time. Later amendments were recorded separately; completed experiments remained unchanged.

\subsection{Ethics and Data Use}
The study analyzed recruitment advertisements without recruiting job applicants. Annotation and review were performed by members of the research team. Institutional ethics approval was not sought, and no formal institutional exemption is claimed. We cannot verify from the retained release records whether employer-contact names, telephone numbers, and email addresses were systematically audited or removed. A removal rate is therefore unavailable. The materials support academic research and peer review under the reuse terms in Data Availability.
