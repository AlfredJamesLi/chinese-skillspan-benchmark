\subsection{Annotation Quality and Inter-Annotator Agreement}
\label{sec:annotation-quality}

Silver-label review and independent annotation assess different aspects of annotation quality. The two assisted-review comparisons below concern generated annotations; the separate blinded study assesses agreement under the final handbook. These designs do not support interpreting their scores as changes in independent agreement over time.

\paragraph{Original Silver post-generation review.}
A separate sample of 100 sentences was reviewed after bulk annotation, excluding the initial 100 reviewed records. Comparing the machine-prefilled annotations with the human-reviewed layer yielded typed exact-span F1 of 0.959. Machine suggestions were visible, so this score measures agreement with assisted human review. \hyperref[app:b]{Appendix B} reports the sampling frame and supporting counts.

\paragraph{Additional review.}
On the additional 150-sentence sample, two reviewers submitted separate annotations with machine suggestions visible. Their pre-adjudication layers yielded typed exact-span F1 of 0.816 (Table~\ref{tab:qa150-pairs}). These assisted-review layers are not used as a new model-evaluation reference.

\paragraph{Blinded annotation protocol.}
Three annotators used handbook v4.2.14 after familiarization on five sentences and practice on 15 sentences, following the training-and-feedback approach discussed by \citet{klie2024annotationquality}. They then independently annotated the same 50-sentence sample from the existing Silver annotation candidates in separate Doccano projects, without access to machine suggestions or one another's labels. The familiarization and practice sentences were excluded from the agreement calculation. The formal sample contained one sentence from each of 50 recorded advertisement identifiers. It was used to assess annotation agreement, not as a held-out model test set, and was not held out from expanded-model training. Appendix~B reports the source counts and sampling limitations; Appendix~D documents overlap with model inputs.

Annotators could consult the handbook and revise their own annotations during individual self-review. We used their latest separate exports after self-review, retaining all 50 sentences and all three annotator pairs. No consensus labels were produced for model evaluation.

\begin{samepage}
\paragraph{Agreement measures.}
We computed typed exact-span F1 for each annotator pair over the full sample and report the mean of the three pairwise scores. A match requires identical boundaries and types, without treating any annotator as the reference~\citep{hripcsak2005agreement}. Boundary-only exact-span F1 ignores type. Character-level agreement was measured using pairwise Cohen's $\kappa$ and three-annotator nominal Krippendorff's $\alpha$, based on L/K/S/T/O assignments to all 2,111 source Unicode code points; O denotes positions outside annotated spans. Reported 95\% percentile intervals were obtained from 10,000 source-stratified sentence-bootstrap samples, retaining all three annotation layers together for each sampled sentence. The intervals are conditional on the observed source composition and the three annotators.\par
\end{samepage}

\begin{samepage}
\paragraph{Agreement results.}
Mean pairwise typed exact-span F1 was 0.818 (95\% CI [0.732, 0.894]), compared with 0.850 for boundary-only exact-span F1 (95\% CI [0.766, 0.923]). The higher boundary-only score indicates additional disagreement over type assignment. Pairwise typed exact-span F1 ranged from 0.762 to 0.805 for K, 0.778 to 0.869 for S, and 0.865 to 1.000 for T. Character-level $\alpha$ was 0.907 (95\% CI [0.851, 0.954]). Character-level coefficients and span-level F1 describe different annotation units and should be interpreted separately~\citep{artstein2008agreement}.\par
\end{samepage}

All three annotators produced identical complete typed-span sets for 34 sentences, including 11 with no annotated spans. They also identified the same two L spans, both in one sentence, providing limited evidence about agreement on this category. The \href{https://github.com/AlfredJamesLi/chinese-skillspan-benchmark/tree/main/reproduction/agreement/finalguide_abc_20261003}{reproduction materials} include the individual annotation exports used in these calculations and the associated diagnostics.

\paragraph{Silver labels against blinded annotations.}
In a post hoc comparison, the adopted expanded pool retained Silver labels for 49 of the 50 blinded-study sentences. Comparing these labels separately with the three blinded annotation layers yielded a mean typed exact-span micro-F1 of 0.709. This measures agreement between historical Silver labels and final-handbook annotations on these sampled texts. \hyperref[app:b]{Appendix B} reports the per-annotator results and the alternative pool.
