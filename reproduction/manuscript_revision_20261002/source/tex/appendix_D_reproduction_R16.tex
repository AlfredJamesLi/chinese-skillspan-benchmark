\subsection{Source Categories and Data Preparation}
The four original collection labels describe acquisition batches; the three acquisition routes identify MacroData, public-institution collection, and Tianchi. They are different classifications. The original training partition contains 10,312 graduate-recruitment and 7,148 AI sentences; development contains 2,143 AI sentences; test contains 1,423 AI, 473 Tianchi/cloud, and 1,341 public-institution sentences. The later expansion uses Tianchi/cloud, public-institution, and listed-company source groups. The blinded agreement sample's 26/16/8 counts refer to these later source groups and cannot be read as proportions of the four original collections.

MacroData's operator is Chongqing Maherui Information Technology Co., Ltd. Public-institution records retain source names, titles, URLs, listed publication dates, and geographic fields where available. Publication dates are not collection dates; a complete crawl-window and source-exclusion history has not been recovered. The authors confirm academic-research use permission. Repository notices separately identify the rights in author-created annotations and the restrictions on third-party text.

B2's 2,319 training/development records contain 2,079 distinct NFC-normalized complete texts. Removing whitespace and trimming edge punctuation gives 2,075 preparation keys; eleven fail the length or punctuation filter, leaving 2,064. Thus the difference of 255 is not entirely duplicated text. These counts describe preparation, not a new deduplicated training experiment.

The preparatory target combined those 2,064 valid keys with 7,936 new candidates. Final assembly used different admission rules and retained 2,077 earlier B2 sentences. The new candidates were processed as batches of 3,620, 1,816, and 2,500. Table~\ref{tab:source-stages} reconciles the final totals; exclusions of 213 and 113 in the first two listed batches, offset by 13 additional earlier records, explain the previously obscured difference of 313. The supplementary alternative differs in the retained channel-comparison batch. Counts do not establish a semantic explanation for every excluded annotation.

The later B2 manifest explicitly specifies sentence-level isolation, and its training, development, and Gold150 file hashes match the evaluated Qwen package. The additional group analysis concerns those later assembled splits; it does not invalidate the original split or the successful reference-sentence exclusion checks.

B2 training contains 1,910 distinct complete texts among 2,150 records. Repeated records remain weighted in training. No new deduplicated training run is implied by this audit, and shared source documents alone do not quantify any effect on model scores.

\subsection{Resource Access and Privacy Screening}
\label{sec:resource-access}
\input{tex/review20260923/access_matrix}

An automated screen examined text fields in 15 public corpus and annotation files, covering 28,635 distinct complete texts. Email, mobile-number, and landline patterns flagged 38, 74, and 50 texts, respectively; categories overlap. No such candidates were found in Gold150 or QA150, but candidates occur in B2 and in the separate agreement sample. These are pattern matches requiring human review, including possible organizational contact details or false positives. The screen does not certify anonymization, cover all names or personal information, or replace review of redistribution permissions. The public audit reports aggregate counts; the masked record-level review list is retained locally for resolution before a privacy-vetted release.

\paragraph{Historical model implementation.}
JobBERT-zh is the project's released Chinese recruitment-domain encoder, with an inherited CRF used in the reported continuation runs. The exact original tokenizer revision, some pretraining/selection settings, and truncation or windowing counts were not recovered. Its 256-token limit is not a character limit; the reference retains all target spans. These gaps constrain reconstruction of historical training, although released predictions can be rescored.
