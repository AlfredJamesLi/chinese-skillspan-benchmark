\subsection{Encoder and Competency-Type Comparisons}
\label{sec:external-benchmark-results}

ESCOXLM-R scored above XLM-R-large under their shared Chinese-supervision protocol, with mean typed exact-span micro-F1 of 0.538 and 0.522, respectively (Table~\ref{tab:external-chinese}, panel A). The paired mean difference was 0.016, with a 95\% interval of $[-0.005, 0.035]$ (Table~\ref{tab:core-paired}). The interval includes zero and does not establish an advantage for ESCO initialisation in this comparison.

Four-type macro-F1 was 0.607 for XLM-R-large and 0.622 for ESCOXLM-R; the K/S/T averages were 0.542 and 0.562. The latter summaries exclude L from the average while retaining the four-type prediction task. Each run of the three trained models in panel B recovered both reference L spans and made one false-positive prediction, yielding $0.800\pm0.000$. Zero standard deviation reflects identical counts across runs; L contains only two reference spans.

Qwen adaptation helped most for T-type competencies, where mean F1 rose from 0.380 to 0.638 (Table~\ref{tab:external-chinese}, panel B; Figure~\ref{fig:qwen-diagnostics}a). Mean F1 increased in all three K/S/T categories. The gains for S and K were 0.148 and 0.210, respectively. Within the three silver-trained configurations in panel B, Qwen had the highest observed means for S and T; ESCOXLM-R had the highest K mean. The three configurations use different prediction heads, training budgets, and inference procedures, so these are observed rankings, not controlled performance claims.
