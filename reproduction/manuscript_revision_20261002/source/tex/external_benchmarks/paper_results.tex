\subsection{Chinese Encoder Baselines and External Comparisons}
\label{sec:external-benchmark-results}

The Chinese comparison does not establish a reliable advantage of ESCO initialization. The encoders achieved mean exact F1 of 0.522 (XLM-R-large) and 0.538 (ESCOXLM-R), with all six prediction files independently rescored against the original reference (Table~\ref{tab:external-chinese}). The mean difference was 0.016; its paired 95\% interval was $[-0.003, 0.034]$. This interval includes zero. Four-type macro-F1 is 0.607 and 0.622; excluding the two-span L class gives descriptive K/S/T macro values of 0.542 and 0.562, respectively. This sensitivity does not replace the stated four-type metric.

The archived run summaries show mixed effects of domain-pretrained initialization across the external tasks (Table~\ref{tab:external-common}). ESCOXLM-R had a higher mean typed exact F1 on SkillSpan skills and Sayfullina and a lower mean on the other six streams. The largest decreases were on SkillSpan knowledge and German ICT; only these two pointwise bootstrap intervals excluded zero (\hyperref[app:e]{Appendix E}, Table~\ref{tab:external-common-ci}). All seeds were retained, including runs selected early in training. FIJO's comparison is based on only 50 test sentences.

The original-task reruns answer related but separate questions. Combining English and Danish training improves Danish classification macro-F1 for both tested encoders. NNOSE retrieval improves the single-seed Green results but not SkillSpan or Sayfullina. \hyperref[app:e]{Appendix E} reports these experiments with the public-subset MaChAmp reruns, separating classification, retrieval, and extraction scores according to their protocols.
