\subsection{External Benchmarks and Multilingual Encoders}
\label{sec:external-benchmark-plan}

To test whether domain-pretrained initialization helps consistently across recruitment tasks, the experiments compare XLM-R-large~\citep{conneau2020xlmr} and ESCOXLM-R~\citep{zhang2023escoxlmr}. They use six public datasets: SkillSpan~\citep{zhang2022skillspan}, Kompetencer~\citep{jensen2022kompetencer}, German ICT~\citep{gnehm2022skills}, Green~\citep{green2022benchmark}, Sayfullina~\citep{sayfullina2018softskills}, and FIJO~\citep{beauchemin2022fijo}. Separate skill and knowledge streams in SkillSpan and Kompetencer give eight extraction tasks, each retaining its original entity types.

Both encoders use fresh linear first-subword BIO heads, AdamW with a learning rate of $2\times10^{-5}$, batch size 8, and a maximum of 20 epochs. Training uses seeds 42, 43, and 44, with development typed exact token-span micro-F1 selecting checkpoints. For the Chinese comparison, both encoders and their new nine-label BIO heads were fine-tuned on B2's 2,150 training and 169 development records. The executed pipeline uses Chinese source characters without translation or an English task head. It labels the first subword of each character and joins predictions from chunks of at most 128 characters, retaining the remainder of long sentences. Tokenizer placeholders preserve positions for whitespace and otherwise discarded characters; scoring uses the original text and offsets. Development F1 selects the checkpoint. Gold150 is used only for diagnostic scoring within these runs, although it was previously used in guideline development. Exact text and identifier checks found no overlap across the three partitions; advertisement-level independence is not established.

The Chinese comparison uses the same optimizer settings, maximum epochs, and three seeds as above. Its linear heads and training budget differ from JobBERT's CRF and Qwen LoRA, so shared-reference scores support descriptive comparisons rather than a controlled ranking of those architectures. We report typed exact and relaxed character-span F1, boundary-only F1, and macro-F1 over L/K/S/T. Macro-F1 gives L, represented by only two reference spans, one quarter of its weight.

For each external task, paired sentence-bootstrap intervals summarize the mean ESCOXLM-R minus XLM-R difference conditional on the three trained seeds. We use 2,000 resamples with seed 20260921 and report training-seed SD separately. The intervals do not account for advertisement clustering or multiple comparisons.

\hyperref[app:e]{Appendix E} gives separate protocols for five-seed MaChAmp-CRF reruns on SkillSpan and German ICT, five-seed Kompetencer classification, and single-seed NNOSE retrieval. These preserve their task-specific scoring: Danish BIO extraction differs from fine-grained classification of supplied spans, and the public German ICT release covers only part of the EDU/EXP/LNG pipeline. The datasets' languages and annotation schemes also differ, so their absolute scores do not measure relative extraction difficulty across languages.

For the Chinese encoder contrast, we additionally resampled the same 150 sentences jointly across both models and all three fixed seeds, using 10,000 draws (seed 20260924). Each draw recomputes per-seed micro-F1 and then averages the paired differences. This post hoc diagnostic interval describes reference-sentence sampling uncertainty; it does not establish population generalization or account for advertisement clustering.
