\subsection{Corpus Construction and Annotation Layers}
\label{sec:challenge-audit}

The source corpus contains 22,840 sentences from four collections: AI, Cloud, public-sector, and graduate recruitment. These names identify collection batches, not mutually exclusive occupational categories or acquisition routes. Its original partition contains 17,460 training, 2,143 development, and 3,237 test sentences. This partition differs from the smaller labeled splits used in the experiments below; the original test partition is not used as a human-annotated test set in this study. Table~\ref{tab:annotation-lineage} summarizes the corpus and its annotation, supervision, and evaluation subsets.

The texts were obtained through three acquisition routes: commercial collections purchased from MacroData~\citep{macrodataPlatform}; public-institution recruitment announcements collected by the research team; and the Recruitment Dataset (ID 163746) on Alibaba Cloud Tianchi~\citep{tianchiRecruitment163746}. For public-institution announcements, team members selected pages from government portals, university and hospital websites, and public recruitment platforms across regions of China. A crawler revisited the selected URLs weekly or monthly. The collected texts were screened and cleaned to form the public-institution subset. \hyperref[app:d]{Appendix D} documents the available source metadata and gaps in the collection history.

We use \emph{original Silver} for the initial pool of generated labels and \emph{expanded Silver} for the later training pools. The initial annotation dataset contains 2,601 records: a human reference set of 150 sentences and 2,451 records with LLM-generated Silver labels. The original Silver pool comprises 100 initially reviewed records, 730 additional records from the model-disagreement queue, and 1,621 historical extraction records. The 980-record disagreement queue supplied the human reference set and the first 830 Silver records.

The human reference set combines 50 calibration sentences with a non-overlapping 100-sentence challenge sample. The calibration sentences were independently annotated from raw text and then adjudicated; the challenge sample was collaboratively annotated and reviewed. Both samples informed guideline development. The finalized labels were frozen for diagnostic evaluation.

The reference set contains 663 spans: 448 S, 125 K, 88 T, and two L. Eight sentences have no annotated span. All 150 complete sentence texts are distinct after NFC normalization. The initial 100-sentence review and the separate 100-sentence post-generation review do not overlap with the reference set, giving 350 unique sentences with human annotation or review at this stage. The historical 15-sentence assisted-review subset is included in this coverage and is not counted separately. Appendices~B~and~D provide the sample counts and sampling details. Figure~\ref{fig:coding-detail} summarizes reference construction, Silver annotation and review, and the separate agreement study.

\paragraph{Expanded supervision and annotation profile.}
The adopted Codex variant contains 9,540 annotation records, combining earlier supervision with additional cloud, public-sector, and listed-company data. Candidate selection prioritized requirement-related keywords. A supplementary 9,646-record pool differs in both annotation channel and retained records. \hyperref[app:d]{Appendix D} documents sampling and exclusions, and \hyperref[app:c]{Appendix C} reports the experiments using these two variants.

The additional review sample comprises 150 newly annotated sentences, stratified into 60 cloud, 45 public-sector, and 45 listed-company sentences. Checks against identifiers and normalized texts excluded the original 350 human-annotated or reviewed sentences. This sample supplements the original 100-sentence post-generation review shown in Figure~\ref{fig:coding-detail}. Historical human annotation and review therefore cover 500 unique sentences, while the human reference set remains the 150-sentence model-evaluation reference. The separate 50-sentence blinded agreement study is described below and is not included in this historical coverage count.

The human reference set was selected from a model-disagreement queue, and its annotation profile differs from that of the supervision data. In the Silver training and development sets used by Qwen and the encoder comparison, 40.4\% of training records and 48.5\% of development records have no annotated span, compared with 5.3\% of reference sentences (Table~\ref{tab:annotation-lineage}). S accounts for 59.7--67.6\% of spans across the five profiled annotation layers. The median span length is four Unicode code points in the reference set and six in these Silver sets. These differences preclude treating the reference set as representative of recruitment sentences. They motivate type- and length-specific diagnostics, but do not by themselves explain exact-versus-relaxed score gaps.
