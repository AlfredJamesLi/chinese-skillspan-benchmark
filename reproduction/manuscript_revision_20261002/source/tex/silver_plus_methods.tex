\subsection{Corpus Construction and Annotation Layers}
\label{sec:challenge-audit}

The corpus contains 22,840 sentences from the AI, Cloud, public-sector, and graduate-recruitment collections, with an original split of 17,460 training, 2,143 development, and 3,237 test sentences. These are collection labels, not mutually exclusive occupations or acquisition routes. The original corpus partition is separate from the smaller labeled experiments below; the 3,237-sentence original test partition is not a human-annotated test set used in this paper. Table~\ref{tab:annotation-lineage} distinguishes the corpus from the smaller subsets used for annotation, supervision, and evaluation.

Texts came from three routes: commercial collections purchased from MacroData~\citep{macrodataPlatform}; public-institution recruitment announcements collected by the research team; and the Recruitment Dataset (dataset ID 163746) on Alibaba Cloud Tianchi~\citep{tianchiRecruitment163746}. For public-institution announcements, team members selected pages from government portals, university and hospital websites, and public recruitment platforms across regions of China. A crawler revisited the registered URLs weekly or monthly. Screening and cleaning produced the public-institution subset. \hyperref[app:d]{Appendix D} records the available source metadata and gaps in the original collection history.

We reserve \emph{original Silver} for the initial generated-label pool and \emph{expanded Silver} for the later training pools. Earlier and revised supervision are denoted B1 and B2 in experiment files, respectively. The original annotation study contains 2,601 records: a 150-sentence human reference, named Gold150 in the released files, and 2,451 records with LLM-generated Silver labels, named Silver-plus in archived files. The Silver set includes 100 initially reviewed records, 730 further records from the model-disagreement queue, and 1,621 historical extraction records. The 980-record disagreement queue contributed the reference sentences and the first 830 Silver records.

The human reference combines 50 calibration sentences with a non-overlapping 100-sentence challenge sample. The calibration sentences were coded independently from raw text and then adjudicated; the challenge sample was collaboratively coded and reviewed. Both informed guideline development. Their final annotations were fixed for evaluation.

The reference contains 663 spans: 448 S, 125 K, 88 T, and two L. Eight sentences contain no annotated span. Its 150 complete sentence texts are distinct after NFC normalization. The two original 100-sentence review samples are separate from the reference, giving 350 unique sentences with human coding or review at this stage. The historical 15-sentence assisted-review subset is nested within this coverage. Appendices~B~and~D give cohort counts and sampling details.

\paragraph{Expanded supervision and annotation profile.}
The adopted Codex pool contains 9,540 sentences from earlier supervision and additional cloud, public-sector, and listed-company collections. Selection prioritized requirement-related keywords. The supplementary 9,646-sentence pool differs in annotation channel and retained records. \hyperref[app:d]{Appendix D} records sampling and exclusions; \hyperref[app:c]{Appendix C} reports the two experiment-specific variants.

Two reviewers checked a further stratified sample of 150 newly annotated sentences, called QA150 in the released summaries. The sample contains 60 cloud, 45 public-sector, and 45 listed-company sentences and excludes the original 350 human-coded or reviewed sentences by identifier and normalized text. Both reviewers completed separate submissions with machine annotations visible. Historical human coding and review thus cover 500 unique sentences; the evaluation reference remains the original 150. The separate 50-sentence blinded agreement study is described below and is not added to this historical coverage count.

The disagreement-selected human reference emphasizes difficult cases and differs from the supervision pool. Empty annotations occur in 40.4\% of original Qwen training records and 48.5\% of development records, compared with 5.3\% of reference sentences (Table~\ref{tab:annotation-lineage}). Occupational skills also dominate all five profiled layers (59.7--67.6\%). Reference spans also have a shorter median length (four code points) than supervision spans (six). These selection and annotation differences preclude treating the reference as a representative sample of recruitment sentences. They motivate type and length diagnostics, but do not by themselves identify the cause of exact-versus-relaxed score gaps. Figure~\ref{fig:length-distributions-r40} shows length and type distributions; \hyperref[app:b]{Appendix B} records the medians.
