\subsection{Corpus Construction and Annotation Layers}
\label{sec:challenge-audit}

The corpus contains 22,840 sentences from the AI, Cloud, public-sector, and graduate-recruitment collections, with an original split of 17,460 training, 2,143 development, and 3,237 test sentences. These collection names describe sources and can overlap in occupational coverage. Table~\ref{tab:annotation-lineage} distinguishes the corpus from the smaller subsets used for annotation, supervision, and evaluation.

Texts came from three routes: commercial collections purchased from MacroData~\citep{macrodataPlatform}; public-institution recruitment announcements collected by the research team; and the Recruitment Dataset (dataset ID 163746) on Alibaba Cloud Tianchi~\citep{tianchiRecruitment163746}. For public-institution announcements, team members selected pages from government portals, university and hospital websites, and public recruitment platforms across regions of China. A crawler revisited the registered URLs weekly or monthly. Screening and cleaning produced the public-institution subset. \hyperref[app:d]{Appendix D} records the available source metadata and gaps in the original collection history.

The original annotation study contains 2,601 records: a 150-sentence human reference, named Gold150 in the released files, and 2,451 records with LLM-generated Silver labels, named Silver-plus. The Silver set includes 100 initially reviewed records, 730 further records from the model-disagreement queue, and 1,621 historical extraction records. The 980-record disagreement queue contributed the reference sentences and the first 830 Silver records.

The human reference combines 50 calibration sentences with a non-overlapping 100-sentence challenge sample. The calibration sentences were coded independently from raw text and then adjudicated; the challenge sample was collaboratively coded and reviewed. Both informed guideline development. Their final annotations were fixed for evaluation.

The reference contains 663 spans: 448 S, 125 K, 88 T, and two L. Eight sentences contain no annotated span. Its 150 complete sentence texts are distinct after NFC normalization. The two original 100-sentence review samples are separate from the reference, giving 350 unique sentences with human coding or review at this stage. The historical 15-sentence assisted-review subset is nested within this coverage. Cohort-level counts and sampling details appear in Appendices B and D.

\paragraph{Expanded supervision and annotation profile.}
The expanded Codex Silver pool contains 9,540 sentences drawn from earlier supervision and additional cloud, public-sector, and listed-company collections. Selection prioritized requirement-related keywords. The alternative annotation channel and inclusion decisions produced a separate 9,646-sentence pool. \hyperref[app:d]{Appendix D} records sampling and exclusions; \hyperref[app:c]{Appendix C} reports the two experiment-specific variants.

Two reviewers checked a further stratified sample of 150 newly annotated sentences, called QA150 in the released summaries. The sample contains 60 cloud, 45 public-sector, and 45 listed-company sentences and excludes the original 350 human-coded or reviewed sentences by identifier and normalized text. Both reviewers completed separate submissions with machine annotations visible. Historical human coding and review thus cover 500 unique sentences; the evaluation reference remains the original 150. The separate 50-sentence blinded agreement study is described below and is not added to this historical coverage count.

The supervision and reference subsets differ substantially in composition. Empty annotations occur in 40.4\% of original Qwen training records and 48.5\% of development records, compared with 5.3\% of reference sentences (Table~\ref{tab:annotation-lineage}). Occupational skills also dominate all five profiled layers (59.7--67.6\%). These differences motivate per-type and empty-sentence diagnostics alongside micro-F1. Figure~\ref{fig:length-distributions-r40} shows length and type distributions; \hyperref[app:b]{Appendix B} records the medians.
