% [R37-MOVE] Counting convention formerly repeated in the corpus chapter.
Character-level Cohen's $\kappa$ adjusts observed agreement for agreement expected by chance from the two coders' marginal label frequencies across the source character positions:
\begin{equation}
\kappa=\frac{p_o-p_e}{1-p_e},
\qquad p_e=\sum_{c\in\mathcal{C}}p_A(c)\,p_B(c).
\label{eq:character-kappa}
\end{equation}
Here, $p_o$ is the fraction of source character positions with identical labels, and $p_A(c)$ and $p_B(c)$ are the two coders' label proportions. The label set $\mathcal{C}$ comprises L/K/S/T and O. For the four-label scheme plus O, the available exports yield $p_o=0.771$ and $p_e=0.471$, giving $\kappa=0.567$. These character-level quantities are distinct from the span counts in Eq.~\eqref{eq:micro-prf}; the 2,571 positions are nested within 50 sentences, not 2,571 independent samples.

Empty--empty sentences contribute no span true positives. Span matching and character-level agreement use different units; reproduction materials document optional category projections.

The post-generation review sample instead compares a machine-prefilled layer with human review: 140 exact typed matches among 144 machine and 148 reviewed spans give P/R/F1 of 0.972/0.946/0.959. Ninety-five sentences have identical complete span sets. The sample was stratified by historical source, empty/nonempty status, and text length from 2,179 eligible records; two small strata totalling 11 records were unsampled. The estimate describes this reviewed sample; it does not provide an unbiased estimate of accuracy across the corpus.

The 15-sentence auditor subset is nested within the post-generation Silver review cohort. Both annotators had access to machine coding suggestions. Their human layers contain the same 27 spans, yielding exact span F1 and character kappa of 1.000. These values describe agreement under machine-assisted review, where shared suggestions can influence both annotators; they are not evidence of independent blind coding or corpus-wide reliability under the final handbook.

The 49 sentences with differing human span sets remain part of the raw agreement calculation. The exported annotations are not replaced by their intersection or a consensus layer. Character-level calculations assign O/L/K/S/T to original Unicode-code-point positions, including spaces and punctuation. Two positions have conflicting types within one reviewer layer and are excluded from both character sequences, leaving $N=5{,}990$ positions. All original spans remain in span-level F1. These raw review layers are not substituted for Gold150; the affected record is identified in the accompanying calculation record. We have not verified final adjudication of these disagreements or traced the resulting decisions into each later training target. The reported coefficients therefore describe the preserved raw review layers. The resulting observed agreement over retained character positions is $p_o=0.934$, with $p_e=0.439$ and $\kappa=0.883$.

For nominal Krippendorff's $\alpha$, let $m_c$ count assignments to category $c$ across both reviewers, with $\sum_c m_c=2N$. The observed and expected disagreements are
\begin{equation}
\alpha=1-\frac{D_o}{D_e},\qquad
D_o=1-p_o,\qquad
D_e=1-\frac{\sum_c m_c(m_c-1)}{2N(2N-1)}.
\label{eq:qa150-alpha}
\end{equation}
Here $D_o=0.066$ and $D_e=0.560$, giving $\alpha=0.883$. The character coefficients and span F1 describe different units of agreement~\citep{artstein2008agreement}; the many O positions contribute to the character calculation. Agreement between two machine suggestion layers likewise measures concordance, not accuracy against an independent human reference. Export hashes, calculation code, and detailed diagnostics accompany the reproduction record.

\paragraph{Independent blinded coding by three annotators.}
The formal sample contains eight listed-company, 16 public-institution, and 26 Tianchi sentences. We used random sampling to select the formal sentences. Each annotator completed familiarization and training before independent coding. The archived exports establish sample membership and source counts, but the complete candidate frame and sampling execution log were not included in this release. The original draw therefore cannot be independently reproduced from the release; source-stratified bootstrap resampling quantifies agreement uncertainty conditional on this sample. Five- and 15-sentence training sets are excluded from formal agreement. The analysis uses pre-discussion labels, not later edited exports. The October 2 reader-edition wording changes did not trigger a new coding round.

The three coders in Table~\ref{tab:quality-main} produced 97/113/106 spans; exact typed matches numbered 69/75/65 for A--B/A--C/B--C. Pairwise F1 ranges are 0.603--0.757 for K, 0.475--0.659 for S, and 0.722--0.837 for T. All coders identified the same two L spans in one sentence. O accounts for 59.5--64.5\% of character positions. The linked reproduction materials retain the training records, freeze receipts, later-edit comparisons, per-type counts, and complete bootstrap specification; none of the 10,000 resamples produced an undefined reported coefficient.
