% [R37-MOVE] Counting convention formerly repeated in the corpus chapter.
Character-level Cohen's $\kappa$ adjusts observed agreement for agreement expected by chance from the two coders' marginal label frequencies across the source character positions:
\begin{equation}
\kappa=\frac{p_o-p_e}{1-p_e},
\qquad p_e=\sum_{c\in\mathcal{C}}p_A(c)\,p_B(c).
\label{eq:character-kappa}
\end{equation}
Here, $p_o$ is the fraction of source character positions with identical labels, and $p_A(c)$ and $p_B(c)$ are the two coders' label proportions. The label set $\mathcal{C}$ comprises L/K/S/T and O. For the four-label scheme plus O, the available exports yield $p_o=0.771$ and $p_e=0.471$, giving $\kappa=0.567$. These coefficients use character positions rather than the spans counted in Eq.~\eqref{eq:micro-prf}. The 2,571 positions are nested within the 50 sampled sentences.

Empty--empty sentences contribute no span true positives. Span matching and character-level agreement use different units; reproduction materials document optional category projections.

The post-generation review compares machine-prefilled annotations with the human-reviewed layer: 140 exact typed matches among 144 machine and 148 reviewed spans give P/R/F1 of 0.972/0.946/0.959. Ninety-five sentences have identical complete span sets. The sample was stratified by historical source, empty/nonempty status, and text length from 2,179 eligible records. The frame excludes 100 initially reviewed records, 100 records reserved for an earlier pilot, and 72 pending records from the 2,451-record original Silver pool; two small strata totalling 11 records were unsampled. The estimate is conditional on this stratified review sample.

The 15-sentence assisted-review subset is nested within the post-generation Silver review cohort. Both annotators had access to machine coding suggestions. Their human layers contain the same 27 spans, yielding exact span F1 and character kappa of 1.000. The shared suggestions may contribute to this agreement between assisted reviewers.

Agreement is computed from both submitted layers, including the 49 sentences with differing span sets. Character-level calculations assign O/L/K/S/T to original Unicode-code-point positions, including spaces and punctuation. Two positions have conflicting types within one reviewer layer and are excluded from both character sequences, leaving $N=5{,}990$ positions. All original spans remain in span-level F1. The accompanying agreement data identify the affected record. Later adjudication and incorporation into training targets have not been verified. Observed agreement over the remaining character positions is $p_o=0.934$, with $p_e=0.439$ and $\kappa=0.883$.

For nominal Krippendorff's $\alpha$, let $m_c$ count assignments to category $c$ across both reviewers, with $\sum_c m_c=2N$. The observed and expected disagreements are
\begin{equation}
\alpha=1-\frac{D_o}{D_e},\qquad
D_o=1-p_o,\qquad
D_e=1-\frac{\sum_c m_c(m_c-1)}{2N(2N-1)}.
\label{eq:qa150-alpha}
\end{equation}
Here $D_o=0.066$ and $D_e=0.560$, giving $\alpha=0.883$. The character coefficients and span F1 describe different units of agreement~\citep{artstein2008agreement}; the many O positions contribute to the character calculation. The machine--machine comparison measures concordance between the suggestion layers. Export hashes and calculation code are available with the reproduction materials.

\subsection{Blinded Coding by Three Annotators}
The annotators comprised one human resource management specialist with a doctorate and two employed master's students in management. The students had sustained annotation experience and three to five years of professional experience. All three contributed to handbook development; two professors advised on the coding process and handbook preparation.

The formal sample contains eight listed-company, 16 public-institution, and 26 Tianchi sentences. The research team reports random selection, with one sentence per advertisement identifier. Each annotator completed familiarization and training before independent coding. The released annotations identify the sampled sentences and sources. The candidate frame and sampling log are unavailable, so the original draw cannot be reproduced. Source-stratified bootstrap intervals quantify agreement uncertainty within this sample. Five- and 15-sentence training sets are excluded from formal agreement. Annotators then individually reviewed their own labels using the handbook, with machine suggestions and other annotators' labels hidden throughout. Mean pairwise typed exact-span F1 was 0.663 before self-review and 0.818 afterward; the analysis uses the latter labels. Both stages are available in the \href{https://github.com/AlfredJamesLi/chinese-skillspan-benchmark/tree/main/reproduction/style_revision_20261005}{reproduction notes}. The historical model reference retains its original labels.

The three coders in Table~\ref{tab:quality-main} produced 104/123/113 spans; exact typed matches numbered 90/92/96 for A--B/A--C/B--C. Pairwise F1 ranges are 0.762--0.805 for K, 0.778--0.869 for S, and 0.865--1.000 for T. All coders identified the same two L spans in one sentence. O accounts for 63.2--64.1\% of character positions. The reproduction materials include individual annotations, per-type counts, and bootstrap code.
