\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

Chinese-SkillSpan makes two annotation decisions explicit: which source characters form a competency mention and which type the mention receives. This allows boundary and type errors to be examined alongside aggregate scores. The different results on silver and human targets show why agreement with generated labels cannot replace human-reference evaluation.

The matched Qwen experiments support a benefit from silver supervision relative to the same unadapted model under fixed inference conditions. The prompted GPT configuration nevertheless retained the highest observed human-reference score, including relative to the expanded-pool Qwen runs.

\subsection{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\textbf{Reference design and label scope.} The human reference set came from a model-disagreement queue and informed handbook development. It therefore supports diagnostic comparisons rather than a development-independent, representative evaluation of recruitment texts. The separate 50-sentence blinded study assesses application of the final handbook, not model performance on a new held-out test set. Its sample and annotation targets differ from those used for model evaluation. K includes qualification and industry-background proxies, while L has only two reference spans. Broader evaluation requires independent annotation of texts from new source documents and separate human review of K subtypes.

\textbf{Label versions and data exposure.} The human reference set, historical supervision, and blinded agreement study use different handbook versions. Agreement under the final handbook does not establish that all historical targets follow its rules; the labels used in completed experiments remain frozen. The original corpus partitions were document-disjoint, and checks on the 2,150/169 Silver training/development sets excluded reference sentences by identifier or exact/NFC-normalized text. Later experimental splits nevertheless share source-document groups. These results therefore do not establish generalization to unseen source documents. Incomplete historical pretraining records also leave prior exposure to evaluation texts uncertain.

\textbf{Experimental scope.} The Qwen comparison measures end-to-end adaptation gains, including changes in output validity; it does not separate gains in content extraction from gains in valid output generation. Prompted configurations differ in inference budgets and access routes. The final expanded-pool comparisons use a single training seed, while the three-seed main experiments provide limited evidence about training variability. Source-group bootstrap intervals account for dependence among sentences from the same recorded source document but remain conditional on the selected reference set and recorded runs. The eight sentences with no reference spans support descriptive false-positive counts, not a population error rate.

\textbf{Future directions.} Within the available annotation budget, this study focuses on flat, single-label spans with explicit source offsets. Future work should prioritize independent evaluation on new source documents and could explore overlapping or multiply labelled spans, together with job titles and document-level context. Applications requiring concise competency descriptions could be addressed through a separate normalization or summarization task linked to supporting source spans. Such extensions would require task-specific annotation and scoring protocols, while retaining the current extraction task as a separate evaluation track.

\subsection{Responsible Use}

The annotations describe requirements in job advertisements, not applicants' abilities. The reported extraction results do not validate the resource for assessing or ranking individual applicants. \hyperref[app:d]{Appendix D} documents the scope of automated privacy screening and the candidates requiring human review.

\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Chinese-SkillSpan provides a benchmark for identifying and typing competency spans in Chinese job advertisements. It combines source-text annotations, separate human reference labels and silver supervision, and an ESCO-informed handbook for span boundaries and context-dependent type assignment. Character offsets, released predictions, and a shared scorer support inspection of boundary and type errors. A separate blinded study assessed independent annotation agreement under the final handbook, distinct from assisted label review.

After individual self-review, the three annotators achieved a mean pairwise typed exact-span F1 of 0.818. Under fixed inference conditions, Qwen adaptation improved end-to-end extraction relative to the same unadapted model, while the prompted GPT configuration had the highest observed typed exact-span micro-F1 on the human reference set. The encoder comparison did not establish an advantage for ESCO initialization under the evaluated protocol. These results support diagnostic use of the benchmark; independent annotation and evaluation on new source documents are needed to assess generalization.
