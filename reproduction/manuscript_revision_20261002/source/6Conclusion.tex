\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

Chinese-SkillSpan makes two annotation decisions explicit: which source characters form a competency mention and which type the mention receives. This allows boundary and type errors to be examined alongside aggregate scores. The different results on silver and human targets show why agreement with generated labels cannot replace human-reference evaluation.

The matched Qwen experiments support a benefit from silver supervision relative to the same unadapted model under fixed inference conditions. The adapted Qwen mean remained below the observed score of the Silver-trained JobBERT-zh + CRF baseline. Prompted GPT retained the highest observed human-reference score. Training and inference settings differ across these model families.

\subsection{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\textbf{Reference design and label scope.} The human reference set came from a model-disagreement queue and informed handbook development. It therefore supports diagnostic comparisons rather than a development-independent, representative evaluation of recruitment texts. The separate 50-sentence blinded study measures agreement among annotators applying the final handbook. All three annotators contributed to handbook development, so their agreement reflects an experienced team's use of the rules. K includes qualification and industry-background proxies, while L has only two reference spans. Broader evaluation requires independent annotation of texts from new source documents and separate human review of K subtypes.

\textbf{Label versions and data exposure.} The human reference set, historical supervision, and blinded agreement study use different handbook versions. Each set of results applies to the label version used in that experiment. The original corpus partitions were document-disjoint, and checks on the 2,150/169 Silver training/development sets excluded reference sentences by identifier or exact/NFC-normalized text. Later experimental splits nevertheless share source-document groups. These results therefore do not establish generalization to unseen source documents. Incomplete historical pretraining records also leave prior exposure to evaluation texts uncertain.

\textbf{Experimental scope.} The Qwen comparison jointly measures output validity and span extraction. Prompted configurations differ in inference budgets and access routes. The final expanded-pool comparisons use a single training seed, while the three-seed main experiments provide limited evidence about training variability. Source-group bootstrap intervals describe variation within this reference set and these model runs. False-positive analysis is limited to eight sentences with no reference spans.

\textbf{Model assistance.} Qwen, DeepSeek, and Kimi were evaluated as baselines and also supplied suggestions during assisted annotation. Shared instructions and machine-assisted review may carry common annotation preferences into the reviewed labels. Complete model-version records are unavailable for some suggestion tools. The blinded annotation study provides complementary evidence with machine suggestions hidden.

\textbf{Future directions.} Within the available annotation budget, this study focuses on flat, single-label spans with explicit source offsets. Future work should prioritize independent evaluation on new source documents and could explore overlapping or multiply labelled spans, together with job titles and document-level context. Applications requiring concise competency descriptions could be addressed through a separate normalization or summarization task linked to supporting source spans. Such extensions would require task-specific annotation and scoring protocols, while retaining the current extraction task as a separate evaluation track.

\subsection{Responsible Use}

The annotations describe requirements in job advertisements, not applicants' abilities. The reported extraction results do not validate the resource for assessing or ranking individual applicants. Qualification and industry-background labels in K describe employer requirements; treating them as measures of applicants' competence could reproduce credential-based or sector-based selection biases. Applicant screening would require a separate fairness and validity assessment. \hyperref[app:d]{Appendix D} documents the scope of automated privacy screening and the candidates requiring human review.

\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Chinese-SkillSpan provides a benchmark for identifying and typing competency spans in Chinese job advertisements. It combines source-text annotations, separate human reference labels and silver supervision, and an ESCO-informed handbook for span boundaries and context-dependent type assignment. Character offsets, released predictions, and a shared scorer support inspection of boundary and type errors. A separate blinded study assessed independent annotation agreement under the final handbook, distinct from assisted label review.

After individual self-review, the three annotators achieved a mean pairwise typed exact-span F1 of 0.818. Under fixed inference conditions, Qwen adaptation improved end-to-end extraction relative to the same unadapted model, while the adapted mean remained below the Silver-trained JobBERT-zh + CRF result and prompted GPT had the highest observed typed exact-span micro-F1 on the human reference set. The encoder comparison did not establish an advantage for ESCO initialization under the evaluated protocol. Together, the resource and evaluation protocol support diagnosis of boundary and type errors under explicit annotation rules.
