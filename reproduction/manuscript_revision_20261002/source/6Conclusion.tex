\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

Silver supervision improved Qwen's end-to-end competency extraction under matched inference conditions. All three adapters improved F1 across K/S/T and recall across the three reference-span length bins (Table~\ref{tab:external-chinese}; Figure~\ref{fig:qwen-diagnostics}). These gains support using silver labels for adaptation to the present annotation task. The adapters also produced fewer responses containing rejected span candidates; the scores jointly reflect span extraction and output validity.

Boundary and type diagnostics make the aggregate scores easier to interpret. The difference between exact and relaxed F1 shows sensitivity to boundary matching, while the sentence examples distinguish boundary mismatches from type disagreements (\hyperref[sec:extraction-diagnostics]{Extraction Diagnostics}). Reporting exact, relaxed, and boundary-only scores together helps assess how predictions align with the annotation scheme. Character offsets let readers locate and inspect disagreements in the source text.

In the single-seed expanded-pool comparison (Table~\ref{tab:expanded-silver-main}), the configuration with the higher score on its own silver test did not have the higher score on the shared human reference. This observation is descriptive and does not establish a ranking across training runs. The silver tests differ in both texts and annotations; the common human reference provides a shared evaluation target.

The present human reference emphasizes ambiguous cases encountered during handbook development. Its coverage also shapes type-specific interpretation: K combines knowledge with qualifications and industry background, and L has only two reference spans. Reference sentences were excluded from Qwen and encoder supervision, although distinct sentences share some source documents (\hyperref[app:d]{Appendix D}). Broader independent annotation on new documents would extend evaluation across requirement types and recruitment contexts. These annotations describe advertised requirements; assessment of individuals would require separate evidence of validity and fairness.

\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Chinese-SkillSpan provides a benchmark for identifying competency spans and assigning their types in Chinese job advertisements. It combines source-text annotations with character-level offsets, an ESCO-informed handbook, separate human reference labels and silver supervision, and shared scoring procedures. In a separate blinded study, the three annotators achieved a mean pairwise typed exact-span micro-F1 of 0.818 after individual self-review.

Under fixed inference conditions, silver-supervised Qwen adaptation improved end-to-end extraction relative to the same unadapted model. Comparisons across annotation targets and diagnostics of boundaries, types, span lengths, and false positives show how the benchmark supports analysis beyond aggregate scores. The released annotations, handbook, predictions, and scoring code provide a basis for inspecting extraction errors and comparing future systems. Broader independent human annotation would enable evaluation on new documents and recruitment contexts.
