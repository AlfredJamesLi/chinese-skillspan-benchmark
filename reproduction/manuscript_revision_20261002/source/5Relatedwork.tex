\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Skill extraction and recruitment datasets.} Recruitment datasets differ in their prediction targets. \citet{sayfullina2018softskills} determine whether a candidate soft-skill phrase refers to an applicant, whereas \citet{bhola2020retrieving} retrieve skills from a predefined inventory at the advertisement level. For span extraction, SkillSpan annotates English job advertisements in two potentially overlapping layers, SKILL and KNOWLEDGE. The dataset covers hard and soft skills, but these are not separate annotation labels~\citep{zhang2022skillspan}. Kompetencer combines manually annotated skill and knowledge spans with ESCO-derived fine-grained labels in Danish and English; its distantly supervised Danish test labels were manually corrected~\citep{jensen2022kompetencer}. \citet{gnehm2022skills} extract spans with coarse EDU/EXP/LNG labels and refine skill areas for taxonomy matching.

Chinese recruitment research includes both forecasting and entity extraction. Job-SDF evaluates skill-demand forecasting at several aggregation levels~\citep{chen2024jobsdf}. For occupational profiling, \citet{cao2021occupational} manually annotated skill and specialty requirements using the BIOES scheme and trained a BERT--BiLSTM--CRF model.

Taxonomy-based weak supervision can reduce manual annotation effort~\citep{zhang2022weak}, but the resulting labels still require validation. \citet{nguyen2024rethinking} report difficulties with coordinated and ambiguous mentions across six recruitment datasets. \citet{senger2024survey} also note the limited availability of public annotations and inconsistent terminology. Chinese-SkillSpan uses flat character-level spans with four competency types. Table~\ref{tab:related-span} compares the prediction units, annotation schemes, and reported materials of selected resources.

\paragraph{Chinese span annotation and competency guidelines.} Chinese NER resources cover social-media text~\citep{peng2015weibo} and fine-grained entity categories~\citep{cluener2020}. Multilingual studies examine entity recognition in noisy text~\citep{multiconer2023} and changes in entity types across contexts~\citep{dynamicner2025}. Their annotation targets differ from the L/K/S/T competency spans considered here. The absence of explicit word delimiters complicates boundary detection in Chinese~\citep{chen2021boundary}; recruitment annotation also requires contextual distinctions among knowledge, occupational actions, and transversal competencies.

ESCO provides a multilingual vocabulary for labor-market information~\citep{levrang2014esco,ESCOv1_2_2024}. SkiLLens combines human involvement with skill extraction and ESCO linking for European job advertisements~\citep{desanto2026skilllens}. Chinese-SkillSpan draws on ESCO's high-level categories for Chinese L/K/S/T span annotation, without assigning ESCO concept identifiers. The handbook specifies when a tool mention should receive S rather than K, when coordinated objects must retain a shared action, and when an experience qualifier belongs within a span. Agreement analysis and adjudication address both span boundaries and type assignments, corresponding to unitization and categorization, respectively~\citep{artstein2008agreement}.

\paragraph{Model-assisted annotation and extraction models.} Large language models (LLMs) can provide initial annotations, although their usefulness depends on task instructions and human validation~\citep{ding2023annotator,li2023coannotating,ulhaq2026nerannotation}. GoLLIE examines how annotation guidelines affect information extraction~\citep{gollie2024}, while \citet{kim2026guidelines} study guideline refinement for LLM annotation. \mbox{JobSkape} generates synthetic job advertisements for skill-to-taxonomy matching~\citep{magron2024jobskape}. Chinese-SkillSpan instead adds model-generated annotations to collected recruitment texts, with documented review of sampled labels. Its annotation prompts follow the handbook version used at each stage.

Encoder-based extraction can combine pretrained models with structured decoding~\citep{devlin2019bert,lafferty2001crf} and continued pretraining on recruitment texts~\citep{gururangan2020dapt,zhang2022skillspan}. The SkillSpan study introduced the domain-adapted JobBERT and JobSpanBERT baselines~\citep{zhang2022skillspan}. ESCOXLM-R uses taxonomy-driven multilingual pretraining~\citep{zhang2023escoxlmr}, and NNOSE retrieves training-data representations to support extraction, including infrequent patterns~\citep{zhang2024nnose}. Adapting these methods to Chinese L/K/S/T extraction requires task-specific protocols because the languages, annotation layers, and data splits differ from those of the original studies.

UIE uses text-to-structure generation for information extraction~\citep{uie2022}, and UniversalNER learns from distilled supervision~\citep{universalner}. GLiNER performs open-type extraction with an encoder~\citep{gliner-naacl24}, whereas GPT-NER uses generative extraction with self-verification~\citep{gptner2025}. These four methods are discussed as related work, not evaluated baselines. \nameref{sec:experiments} details evaluated models, supervision, and scoring procedures.
