\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:experiments}

We compare prompted extraction, adaptation with silver supervision, and taxonomy-informed encoder initialization using a common human reference set and shared scoring rules. The Qwen adaptation and encoder-initialization comparisons hold their respective inference or training protocols fixed; supplementary experiments examine two expanded silver pools.

\subsection{Data and Splits}

The 150-sentence human reference set described in \nameref{sec:framework} provides the evaluation labels for all main model comparisons. JobBERT-zh + CRF uses 2,156 silver training records and 169 development records. Qwen and the encoder comparison use 2,150 training records and the same 169 development records after removing six training records whose complete texts matched three development texts after NFC normalization.

In the 2,150/169 split, training and development share no complete sentences, either as originally stored or after NFC normalization. Human-reference sentences are also excluded by identifier and normalized text. Different sentence records in these sets can nevertheless come from the same source document. \hyperref[app:d]{Appendix D} gives the original corpus partitions, sentence-level checks, and source-document overlap counts.

Supplementary Qwen experiments use the adopted and alternative expanded silver pools. Each uses sentence-level training, validation, and test splits; shared records retain the same split assignments across pools. Validation labels determine checkpoint selection, and silver-test scores measure agreement with generated annotations. Both configurations are also evaluated on the common human reference, whose sentences are excluded from fitting and model selection. The pools differ in annotation channel and included records; \hyperref[app:c]{Appendix C} gives their split sizes and settings.

\subsection{Models and Comparisons}

Prompted inference compares GPT-5.6 Terra, Claude Sonnet 4.5, DeepSeek-V4-Pro, Kimi K2.6, and Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct~\citep{qwen2025report} under shared annotation instructions. DeepSeek is evaluated with and without thinking, giving six prompted configurations. Table~\ref{tab:model-identity} lists access routes and inference settings. GPT and Claude are accessed through APIs; their names follow the service responses. Request and response identifiers are documented in the reproduction materials linked in \hyperref[app:c]{Appendix C}.

To assess silver-supervised adaptation, we compare Qwen with and without a project-trained LoRA adapter~\citep{hu2022lora}, using the same base checkpoint and inference procedure. JobBERT-zh + CRF provides a supervised sequence-labeling baseline that combines an encoder pretrained on Chinese recruitment text with an inherited CRF head.

\subsection{Evaluation Metrics}

The primary metric is typed exact-span micro-F1, which requires identical span boundaries and L/K/S/T type. Typed relaxed-span micro-F1 allows boundary variation through one-to-one greedy matching of same-type spans with intersection-over-union (IoU) of at least 0.5:

\begin{equation}
\mathrm{IoU}(u,v)=\frac{|u\cap v|}{|u\cup v|},
\qquad \mathrm{IoU}(u,v)\geq 0.5.
\label{eq:span-iou}
\end{equation}

Here, $u$ and $v$ are the sets of Unicode-code-point positions in the predicted and reference spans; $\lvert\cdot\rvert$ counts positions. Under either matching rule, true positives (TP) are matched prediction--reference pairs, false positives (FP) are unmatched predictions, and false negatives (FN) are unmatched reference spans. Summing counts over sentences gives:

\begin{equation}
P_{\mathrm{micro}}=\frac{\mathrm{TP}}{\mathrm{TP}+\mathrm{FP}},
\quad R_{\mathrm{micro}}=\frac{\mathrm{TP}}{\mathrm{TP}+\mathrm{FN}},
\quad F_{1,\mathrm{micro}}=\frac{2\mathrm{TP}}{2\mathrm{TP}+\mathrm{FP}+\mathrm{FN}}.
\label{eq:micro-prf}
\end{equation}

All 150 reference sentences are scored. When some span candidates in a response are rejected, its accepted spans are still scored. Malformed responses and responses with no accepted spans are treated as empty predictions. Sentences with neither predicted nor reference spans contribute no span counts, and a metric with a zero denominator is set to zero. \hyperref[app:c]{Appendix C} details the parser, matching rules, and scoring code.

Boundary-only exact-span micro-F1 matches boundaries while ignoring type. Per-type scores use all reference sentences, including those without a reference span of the evaluated type. Macro-F1 averages category-level F1. Models predict four types, and the encoder comparison reports both L/K/S/T and K/S/T averages; the latter omits the two-span L category from the summary.

Main three-seed experiments are summarized by their mean and sample standard deviation. Prompted configurations and expanded-pool experiments are single runs unless stated otherwise. Paired model comparisons use 95\% percentile intervals from 10,000 bootstrap resamples of the 61 source-document groups in the human reference. Each draw samples complete groups with replacement and is shared by all compared models. These intervals describe variation from resampling reference documents for fixed model runs; seed standard deviations describe variation across training runs. \hyperref[app:c]{Appendix C} gives the full calculation.

\subsection{Prompted Inference on the Human Reference Set}

GPT-5.6 Terra achieved the highest observed typed exact-span micro-F1 among six configurations, scoring 0.667 compared with 0.647 for DeepSeek without thinking (Figure~\ref{fig:model-comparison}a; Table~\ref{tab:gold150-shared-r7}). The paired F1 difference was 0.020, with a 95\% confidence interval of $[-0.027, 0.061]$ obtained by bootstrapping source-document groups (Table~\ref{tab:core-paired}). Because the interval includes zero, it does not provide clear evidence of a performance advantage for GPT.

The DeepSeek configurations differed in precision and recall. With thinking, precision was higher (0.713 versus 0.651), but recall was lower (0.505 versus 0.643), with lower exact-span F1. Their inference budgets differed. Unadapted Qwen had the lowest recall (0.281).

\subsection{Extraction Diagnostics}
\label{sec:extraction-diagnostics}

The recall gains from Qwen adaptation extended across all three reference-span length bins: $\leq4$, 5--8, and $\geq9$ Unicode code points (Figure~\ref{fig:qwen-diagnostics}b). Each adapter improved exact-match recall in every bin, which contained 346, 243, and 74 reference spans, respectively.

Adaptation also reduced false-positive spans on the eight sentences with no reference spans (Figure~\ref{fig:qwen-diagnostics}c,d). The unadapted model produced 19 false-positive spans in two sentences, compared with 6, 4, and 0 spans for seeds 42, 43, and 44. The corresponding numbers of affected sentences were two, one, and zero. For seed 42, fewer false-positive spans occurred in the same two sentences, so the number of sentences containing false positives did not change.

The unadapted Qwen predictions illustrate how boundaries and types affect scoring. Applying relaxed matching to the same predictions raised F1 from 0.361 to 0.470 (Table~\ref{tab:gold150-shared-r7}). Under exact matching, the 181 false positives comprised 27 same-boundary/different-type pairs, 89 same-type/overlapping-boundary pairs, and 65 other unmatched predictions. These groups describe relationships to the reference annotations. Overlapping boundaries alone do not guarantee a relaxed match, which also requires the same type and an IoU of at least 0.5.

Two examples illustrate these patterns. In the first, Qwen extended an S span meaning ``technical project development planning'' to include surrounding action and object wording; its overlap with the reference remained below the relaxed threshold. In the second, Qwen assigned S to the word meaning ``method'', while the reference assigned K to the same span. Both pairs therefore failed exact and relaxed typed matching. \hyperref[sec:error-examples]{Appendix C} provides the complete Chinese sentences, predicted and reference spans, offsets, and sentence identifiers.

\subsection{Evaluation with Expanded Silver Pools}
\label{sec:expanded-pool-results}

The two expanded-pool Qwen configurations changed order across silver and human-reference evaluation. With seed 42, the adopted pool yielded F1 of 0.809 on its silver test set and 0.585 on the common human reference; the alternative yielded 0.759 and 0.597, respectively (Table~\ref{tab:expanded-silver-main}). Thus, the configuration with higher agreement on its generated test labels had lower agreement on the shared human reference. The pool-specific silver tests differ in both texts and labels, so their score gap does not isolate annotation quality or the effect of the annotation channel.

The \hyperref[sec:annotation-quality]{annotation-quality analysis} provides a separate comparison of silver labels against blinded human annotations on common sentences. Table~\ref{tab:source-stages} gives the constituent batches and final counts for the expanded pools. Exploratory expanded JobBERT runs in \hyperref[app:c]{Appendix C} scored below the main baseline, with supervision, source composition, and model selection changed together.
