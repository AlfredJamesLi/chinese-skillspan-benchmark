% [R37-MOVE] Counting convention formerly repeated in the corpus chapter.
Character-level Cohen's $\kappa$ adjusts observed agreement for agreement expected from the two coders' marginal label frequencies:
\begin{equation}
\kappa=\frac{p_o-p_e}{1-p_e},
\qquad p_e=\sum_{c\in\mathcal{C}}p_A(c)\,p_B(c).
\label{eq:character-kappa}
\end{equation}
Here, $p_o$ is the fraction of source character positions with identical labels, and $p_A(c)$ and $p_B(c)$ are the two coders' label proportions. The label set $\mathcal{C}$ comprises L/K/S/T and O. For the four-label scheme plus O, the available exports yield $p_o=0.771$ and $p_e=0.471$, giving $\kappa=0.567$. These character-level quantities are distinct from the span counts in Eq.~\eqref{eq:micro-prf}; the 2,571 positions are nested within 50 sentences, not 2,571 independent samples.

Empty--empty sentences contribute no span true positives. Span matching and character-level agreement use different units; optional category projections are documented in the reproduction materials.

The post-generation review sample instead compares a machine-prefilled layer with human review: 140 exact typed matches among 144 machine and 148 reviewed spans give P/R/F1 of 0.972/0.946/0.959. Ninety-five sentences have identical complete span sets. The sample was stratified by historical source, empty/nonempty status, and text length from 2,179 eligible records (seed 20260908); two small strata totalling 11 records were unsampled. The estimate describes this reviewed sample, not an unbiased corpus-wide accuracy estimate.

The initial review and post-generation review retain separate versioned annotation layers. The source of the initial review records and each completed run's label version are documented separately.

The September 17 Dual15 export records two separate annotation queues (collaborative annotation disabled), with both coders confirming all 15 sentences. Across 719 source code points, both coders assign 57 K, 144 S, 4 T, 514 O, and no L labels. Thus $p_o=1.000$, $p_e=0.557$, and Cohen's $\kappa=1.000$; all 27 typed spans also match exactly (F1 1.000). The calculation uses the same character-label convention as the 50-sentence comparison.

Separate queues establish separate submissions; the coefficient characterizes this auditor-subset agreement and is not treated as blind raw-text reliability. The 49 sentences with differing human span sets remain part of the raw agreement calculation. The exported annotations are not replaced by their intersection or a consensus layer. Character-level calculations assign O/L/K/S/T to original Unicode-code-point positions, including spaces and punctuation. Two positions have conflicting types within one reviewer layer and are excluded from both character sequences, leaving $N=5{,}990$ positions. All original spans remain in span-level F1. The conflicts occur in the raw review layer for \texttt{sp10k-01837}; these layers are not substituted for Gold150. The available records do not establish a final adjudication or record-level propagation into each later training target. This unresolved provenance is separate from the documented raw-layer agreement calculation. The resulting observed agreement is $p_o=0.934$, with $p_e=0.439$ and $\kappa=0.883$.

For nominal Krippendorff's $\alpha$, let $m_c$ count assignments to category $c$ across both reviewers, with $\sum_c m_c=2N$. The observed and expected disagreements are
\begin{equation}
\alpha=1-\frac{D_o}{D_e},\qquad
D_o=1-p_o,\qquad
D_e=1-\frac{\sum_c m_c(m_c-1)}{2N(2N-1)}.
\label{eq:qa150-alpha}
\end{equation}
Here $D_o=0.066$ and $D_e=0.560$, giving $\alpha=0.883$. The character coefficients and span F1 describe different units of agreement~\citep{artstein2008agreement}; the many O positions contribute to the character calculation. Agreement between two machine suggestion layers likewise measures concordance, not accuracy against an independent human reference. Export hashes, calculation code, and detailed diagnostics accompany the reproduction record.
